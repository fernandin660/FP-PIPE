Many businesses launch a website and wait for customers to arrive. A few weeks later they search for their own name on Google and find nothing. It isn't bad luck: Google needs a few signals before it can discover a site, understand it and trust it. The good news is that most of them are within reach of any small business.

## 1. Google has to know the site exists

A new website doesn't appear on its own. Google finds pages by following links and reading the lists a site provides. Three things speed this up a lot:

- **Google Search Console**: Google's free tool for site owners. It's where you prove the site is yours, see which pages have been included and ask Google to visit a new page.
- **A sitemap**: a file listing your important pages. You submit its address in Search Console.
- **Links from elsewhere**: your Instagram profile, LinkedIn page and Google Business Profile pointing to the site.

Even when everything is right, Google doesn't promise a timeline. For a new site it usually takes anywhere from a few days to a few weeks before the first pages show up.

## 2. The technical side mustn't get in the way

You don't need to code, but whoever builds the site should get the basics right:

- **Fast loading on mobile.** Most searches for local businesses happen on a phone.
- **A secure address (https)**, with the padlock in the browser.
- **A title and a description for every page**: that's what appears in search results.
- **Real text on the page**, not just images with words in them. Google reads text.
- **Internal pages kept out of search**: login areas, customer dashboards and test pages shouldn't appear on Google.
- **Languages clearly marked**: if the site has an English and a French version, each page should say which language it's in and where its counterpart lives.

Then there's "structured data": information hidden in the code that tells Google, in an organised way, your business name, what you do, phone number, address and prices. Visitors don't see it, but it helps Google and AI tools understand your business.

## 3. Content that answers the customer's question

This matters most, and it's where most sites go wrong. Google wants to show the page that best solves what the person searched for. So:

- **Write about what customers ask**, not what sounds nice: "how much does it cost", "open on Saturdays", "do you deliver", "can I order for an event".
- **Be specific.** "Custom wedding cakes from £X" says far more than "quality and tradition".
- **Show real experience**: photos of your work, examples, answers only someone in the trade could give.
- **Avoid generic copy.** Pages churned out in bulk, copied, or stuffed with repeated keywords to "trick Google" are treated as spam and can drag the whole site down.

Using AI to help you write isn't forbidden. The problem is publishing text that hasn't been reviewed, has nothing original in it and doesn't help the reader.

## 4. For local businesses: your Google profile counts a lot

If you serve a local area, your **Google Business Profile** needs to be complete:

- name, address and phone number **exactly the same** as on the site;
- opening hours kept up to date, including public holidays;
- the right category and real photos;
- a link to the site;
- replies to reviews, negative ones included, written politely.

The site and the profile reinforce each other: when the details match, Google trusts both more.

## 5. Signals of trust beyond your site

Google also looks at what the rest of the web says about you:

- social profiles linking to the site;
- mentions in industry directories, local business associations and local press;
- customers and partners who mention your business with a link.

One link from a respected site in your field is worth more than dozens from unknown ones. Buying links is risky and can lead to penalties.

## 6. What about AI search?

ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's own AI answers also recommend businesses. The logic is similar: they rely on sites they can read, with clear information that other sources confirm. A site built properly for Google is already largely ready for AI.

One caveat: some hosting and security services block these tools' crawlers by default. Check with whoever manages your site what's allowed.

## Summary

1. Add the site to Google Search Console and submit the sitemap.
2. Make sure it loads quickly on mobile and every page has a title and description.
3. Write pages that answer customers' real questions.
4. Complete your Google Business Profile with the same details as the site.
5. Connect your social profiles and partners to the site.
6. Track results in Search Console and keep improving.
